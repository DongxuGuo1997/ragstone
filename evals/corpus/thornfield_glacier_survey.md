# Thornfield Glacier Mass-Balance Survey: 2019-2023 Field Campaigns

Dr. Mirela Vasquez and Dr. Tomas Lindqvist
Polar Research Unit, Kestrel Institute
Published March 2024

## Abstract

This report presents five years of mass-balance observations from the
Thornfield Glacier, a temperate valley glacier in the Halvard Range. Over
the 2019-2023 campaigns the glacier terminus retreated 340 meters, with an
average annual mass loss of 1.2 meters water equivalent. We describe the
stake network, the seasonal measurement protocol, and the drivers behind
the accelerating loss observed in the final two seasons.

## Methodology

The survey maintained a network of 17 ablation stakes distributed between
the terminus at 1,480 meters elevation and the upper accumulation zone at
2,650 meters. Stake positions were resurveyed at the start and end of each
melt season using differential GPS.

Stake-array calibration followed the method of Okafor and Brandt (2015),
adjusted for the crevasse density of the lower icefall. Snow density in
the accumulation zone was sampled in late April of each year from four
snow pits, following the automated ablation-sensing cross-checks
introduced by Castellanos, Uusitalo, and Devi (2021).

## Findings

Terminus retreat totaled 340 meters across the survey period, with more
than half of that loss (185 meters) occurring in the 2022 and 2023 seasons
alone. The average annual mass balance was -1.2 meters water equivalent,
ranging from -0.7 in 2019 to -1.9 in 2023.

Meltwater discharge in the proglacial stream peaked in August of every
survey year. The 2023 peak discharge was 40 percent above the five-year
mean, coinciding with a record-low winter snowpack.

The equilibrium line altitude rose from approximately 2,150 meters in 2019
to 2,410 meters in 2023, leaving less than a fifth of the glacier surface
in the accumulation zone by the end of the survey.

## Data Availability

Stake records and GPS survey files are archived by the Kestrel Institute
and available on request from the Polar Research Unit.
