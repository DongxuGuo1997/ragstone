# The Aurora-9 Robotic Sample-Return Mission

Aurora-9 is the robotic follow-up to the crewed Aurora-7 expedition. Unlike
its predecessor, Aurora-9 carries no crew: the spacecraft is fully
autonomous, overseen by a small flight team at the Tanami operations
center.

## Launch and Target

Aurora-9 launched on June 2, 2034, also from the Woomera complex. Its
target is asteroid 2030 QF-4, nicknamed "Thistle" — a carbonaceous body
roughly twice the diameter of Bramble, the asteroid visited by Aurora-7.

## Spacecraft

The vehicle reuses the Vela Drive architecture from Aurora-7, but in an
upgraded twin-cluster configuration producing 7.6 newtons of continuous
thrust. Power comes from a single deployable solar wing of 140 square
meters paired with a small radioisotope unit for operations in shadow.

## Mission Profile

The outbound cruise to Thistle is planned at 156 days, considerably faster
than Aurora-7's journey thanks to the higher thrust and a favorable launch
window. After arrival, the spacecraft will spend three months mapping the
asteroid before deploying two landers.

## Sampling Plan

Aurora-9 is designed to return 1,200 grams of material — more than the
850 grams its crewed predecessor brought back — collected from two
separate sites on opposite hemispheres of Thistle. Samples will return to
Earth in a passive entry capsule targeted at the Woomera recovery range.

## Program Cost

The Aurora-9 program is budgeted at 980 million dollars, less than half
the cost of Aurora-7, reflecting the savings of an uncrewed design and
reuse of flight-proven hardware.
