# The Aurora-7 Asteroid Sample-Return Mission

Aurora-7 was an international crewed deep-space mission that launched on
March 14, 2031 from the Woomera spaceflight complex in South Australia. The
mission's objective was to rendezvous with asteroid 2027 KX-11, informally
nicknamed "Bramble" by the navigation team, collect surface material, and
return it to Earth for laboratory analysis.

## Crew

The mission carried a crew of four. It was commanded by Dr. Ingrid Halvorsen,
a Norwegian planetary geologist who had previously served two rotations on
the Gateway lunar station. The pilot was Commander Rafael Ostos, the flight
engineer was Dr. Mei-Lin Zhao, and the mission specialist responsible for
sampling operations was Dr. Kwame Asante. The designated backup commander,
who trained with the crew for two years but did not fly, was Marcus Oyelaran.

## Spacecraft and Propulsion

The Aurora-7 vehicle consisted of a crew module, a service module, and a
detachable sampling lander. Primary propulsion was provided by the Vela
Drive, a xenon-fed ion engine cluster producing a continuous thrust of
4.2 newtons. Although this thrust is small, the engine operated for months
at a time, making the long transfer practical. Electrical power came from
two deployable solar wings with a combined area of 210 square meters.

## The Journey

The outbound journey from Earth to asteroid Bramble took 224 days. During
cruise, the crew conducted thirty-one scheduled science activities,
including a long-duration study of cosmic ray exposure. The spacecraft
arrived at the asteroid on October 24, 2031 and spent six weeks in proximity
operations, mapping the surface at a resolution of five centimeters before
the sampling lander descended.

## Sampling and Return

The sampling lander touched down in a smooth basin designated Site Juniper.
Using a rotary coring tool, the crew collected a total of 850 grams of
regolith and rock fragments, exceeding the mission requirement of
500 grams. The samples were sealed in nitrogen-purged canisters for the
return trip. Aurora-7 returned to Earth orbit the following year, and the
sample canisters were transferred to the Tanami Curation Facility.

## Program Cost and Legacy

The total program cost of Aurora-7 was 2.3 billion dollars, shared across
five space agencies. The mission demonstrated long-duration crewed ion
propulsion for the first time, and material from Site Juniper provided the
first direct evidence of hydrated minerals on a stony asteroid of the
KX class. A follow-up robotic mission, Aurora-8, is under study.
