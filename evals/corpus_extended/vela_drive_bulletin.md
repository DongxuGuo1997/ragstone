# Vela Drive Ion Engine: Maintenance Bulletin

This bulletin provides essential maintenance and operational guidelines for the Vela Drive ion engine, used in the Aurora-7 and Aurora-9 missions. Adhering to the following procedures ensures optimal performance and longevity of the engine.

## Engine Specifications

The Vela Drive is a high-efficiency ion propulsion system designed for deep-space missions. It operates by ionizing xenon gas and accelerating the ions to produce thrust. The engine is available in two configurations: the single-thruster Vela-4.2 and the twin-cluster Vela-7.6.

- **Vela-4.2**: Used in the Aurora-7 mission, this configuration delivers a thrust of 4.2 newtons. It consumes xenon at a rate of 0.5 milligrams per second.
- **Vela-7.6**: Deployed in the Aurora-9 mission, this twin-cluster configuration provides a combined thrust of 7.6 newtons with a xenon consumption rate of 1.1 milligrams per second.

Both configurations feature a specific impulse of 3,200 seconds and operate within a temperature range of -50°C to 150°C.

## Service Intervals

Routine maintenance is critical for the Vela Drive's sustained performance. The following service intervals must be observed:

- **Every 12 months**: Inspect ionization chamber and replace the xenon feed line filter (part number XF-12). Check for any signs of wear or contamination.
- **Every 24 months**: Conduct a full diagnostic test using the Vela Diagnostic Kit (VDK-24). This includes checking the electrical connections, ion thruster alignment, and xenon flow rate calibration.
- **Every 36 months**: Replace the ion accelerator grid (part number AG-36) to prevent efficiency loss due to grid erosion.

## Xenon Consumption and Refueling

The Vela Drive's xenon consumption rates are critical for mission planning and engine operation. The xenon tanks should be refueled according to the mission's duration and thrust requirements.

- **Aurora-7**: The mission consumed approximately 38.8 kilograms of xenon over its 224-day journey.
- **Aurora-9**: With its higher thrust, the mission required 94.6 kilograms of xenon for its operational period.

Refueling should be conducted in a controlled environment to prevent contamination. Use only certified xenon gas (purity 99.999%) and ensure all connections are secure before initiating the refueling process.

## Fault Codes and Troubleshooting

The Vela Drive is equipped with an advanced diagnostic system that reports faults via the onboard display and the VelaLink app. Common fault codes include:

- **V-101** — Xenon flow anomaly. Indicates a deviation in the expected xenon flow rate. Check the xenon feed line and replace the filter if necessary.
- **V-204** — Ionization chamber temperature out of range. Ensure the thermal control system is functioning correctly and adjust the cooling parameters as needed.

## Firmware Updates

Firmware updates for the Vela Drive are distributed through the VelaLink app. It is recommended to check for updates every 90 days. Updates are applied automatically during the spacecraft's scheduled downtime to avoid interrupting propulsion operations. The system retains the previous firmware version for rollback in case of update issues.

By following these guidelines, operators can ensure the Vela Drive ion engine remains in peak condition, supporting successful mission outcomes.
